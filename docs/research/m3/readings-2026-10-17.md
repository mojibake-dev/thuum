# M3's first lab readings (2026-10-17, the engine alone)

Four solo runs on c1 (no skymp5-client), SkyrimSE 1.6.1170, lab driver main
5801c6cf. A first set (20261017-075909 to -080655) ran paused under Survival
Mode's first prompt and measured nothing; these reruns ran live (actors
moving, game time advancing). The drafts are in drafts/.

## x-npc-puppet (20261017-082859): what EnableAI(false) leaves of an NPC

A bandit 150 units ahead. Against the engine read (re-ai-puppet.md):

- AI on: its head moved 7.3 units in 0.7 s, alive. The power-attack idle was
  refused both times, with AI on and off: its sword stayed sheathed (the
  idle's own conditions; the reference melee-power-b plays it only on a
  bandit in combat), so the freeze across an idle is unread.
- AI off: aiEnabled false; head and position bit-identical across reads.
  The graph froze at rest, as the read predicts.
- Unrelenting Force's first word on the AI-off bandit: four hits taken
  (health 35 to 33), its graph took staggerStart and IsStaggering went true,
  but its head did not move: the stagger was taken, not played. Its
  position slid away from the push at about 39 units a second, 122 units in
  4.4 s, the bones not following: the movement step is not gated by the AI
  bit, as the read found. It went into combat against the player with AI
  off: the combat manager is not gated either.
- AI on again: the waiting stagger played, the model caught up with its
  position (the head moved 195 units), and it stayed in combat against the
  player, sword drawn.

So the read's candidate set is needed whole, not EnableAI alone: AI off,
the puppet put back into the animation pass (or relayed actions and
staggers never play), the movement step skipped (or the engine slides it),
and combat stopped on demotion and on combat events (or a hit drafts it
into a fight). Going live carries over what it took while puppet (the
stagger, the combat target), which ADR-035 point 7's promote has to set or
clear on purpose.

## r-npc-packages (20261017-083112): Riverwood's own NPCs

From about 7:56 for 90 s: Gerdur walked south and west, idled, walked on;
Hod came off the mill platform, turned back about 1.5 to 3 s after the

## r-npc-fight (20261017-083447): two bandits against the player

Both fought before the console's startcombat: attacks, two power attacks
(one refused), blocks, seven hits on the player, two player staggers, then
both circled at 126 to 171 units in move-and-turn cycles.

## r-cell-leave (20261017-083700): a cell left and re-entered

The clock read 8.36 on the return, about 20 game minutes in the inn. The
engine moved Hod while the player was away: he was walking south before and
stood on the mill platform after, the same spot r-npc-packages saw him
settle on, a trip of about 33 s live there in at most about 22 s here. The
engine keeps a left cell's schedules going; ADR-035 freezes a cell nobody is
in. That difference is the proposal's choice, now measured, not an
accident.

## What the tools could not see, to fix before the next references

- The recorder hooks graph events only for 0xFF actors and the player, and
  actions only for the player (lab/driver/src/index.ts:708-715, :760), so
  Riverwood's own NPCs show no events or actions; a paused-game guard keyed
  on events would misread such runs.
- npc-state's package read showed 0x3EAB9 at every read, AI on or off: it
  cannot show a package stopping or changing.
- Tracks cap at 240 samples of 250 ms (index.ts:237), 62 s of a 90 s watch.
- The console's startcombat paused the game for about 3.5 s inside the
  watch; a driver step should start combat instead.
- The save arrives near 8:21, so setting the hour to 7.9 turns the clock
  back across 8:00; and the watch's random actor search catches a different
  set each time, so a reference should name its actors by form id.
- Every assert checks nothing.

### Addendum, 2026-10-18 (thuum branch m3-driver-recorder)

The list above is answered on that branch, to be proven by its first
reference run: watch-start names actors by placed form id ({formIds},
each with its own animation-event hook filtered to its id) and takes the
track's cap ({trackMax}); npc-ai and npc-state take {formId}; a
start-combat step replaces the console's startcombat; a reference reads a
named actor with watched_id(<id>) and no server position. The drafts here
use them: r-npc-packages names Riverwood's fourteen residents by reference
(riverwood-ids.md: every one a persistent ACHR in its home interior, walked
outside by its package), r-npc-fight starts the fight through the step. The
package read (gap 2) is still the same Papyrus getter; naming the actor is
what lets a reference poll it across a schedule's switch. The actions half
of gap 1 (a cell's NPC's BGSActions) stays open: the actionPerformed hook
is filtered to the player in native and its event carries no actor id.
